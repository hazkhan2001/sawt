# Sawt: permission requests

Five letters. Send them before writing any more code, because these take weeks and
everything else takes hours. Each one is short on purpose: archivists read a lot of
requests, and the ones that get answered say quickly who you are, exactly what you
want, and what you are offering in return.

Three things every letter does deliberately:

1. **Names specific holdings.** "Your collection" reads as a mass mailing. "The
   Snouck Hurgronje cylinders recorded in Jeddah, 1906 to 1909" reads as someone who
   did the work.
2. **Offers attribution and a link back.** Archives exist to be used and cited. Say
   how you will credit them, in the letter, before they ask.
3. **Asks a narrow, answerable question.** Not "may I use your archive" but "may I
   stream these items, with credit, on a non-commercial map." A yes/no question gets
   a yes or a no. An open question gets silence.

Sign-off is **Hamza** throughout.

## 1. Leiden University Libraries — Snouck Hurgronje Jeddah cylinders

**Route:** Leiden University Libraries, Special Collections. Use the "Ask a
librarian" / Special Collections contact form at library.universiteitleiden.nl, or
write to the Middle Eastern collections curator. These recordings sit in the
Oriental Manuscripts and Rare Books holdings.

**Subject:** Permission request: Snouck Hurgronje Jeddah cylinder recordings (1906–1909)

Dear Special Collections team,

I am building a non-commercial online map of recorded Islamic musical and devotional
traditions, called Sawt. It places historic and field recordings on a globe so that
listeners can hear a tradition alongside a short written account of where it comes
from. The project is self-funded, carries no advertising, and is not a commercial
product.

I am writing about the wax cylinder recordings made by Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje in
Jeddah between 1906 and 1909, which I understand Leiden holds. They are, as far as I
can establish, among the earliest recordings of music and recitation from the Hijaz,
and there is nothing comparable available under an open licence.

I understand these were never commercially published and so remain in copyright in
the United States regardless of their age. I am not asking to download or redistribute
the files. My question is narrower: would the Libraries consider permitting a small
number of excerpts to be streamed within this map, credited to Leiden University
Libraries with a link back to your catalogue record for each item?

If a licence fee or a formal agreement applies, I would be glad to hear the terms. If
streaming is not possible, I would still welcome guidance on whether any of this
material has been cleared for public listening elsewhere.

With thanks for your time,

Hamza

## 2. AMAR Foundation — Arab music archiving and research

**Route:** amar-foundation.org (contact page). The foundation is based in Lebanon and
was established by Kamal Kassar around a large collection of 78rpm discs and early
recordings of Arab art music.

**Subject:** Permission request: early Arab art music recordings for a non-commercial heritage map

Dear AMAR Foundation,

I am building Sawt, a non-commercial online map of Islamic musical and devotional
traditions. Listeners click a place and hear a recording from it, with a short written
account of the tradition, the mode, and the performer where that is known.

The gap I keep running into is exactly what AMAR exists to preserve. Searching the
open-licence archives thoroughly, I have found a great deal of adhan and field
recording and almost nothing of the maqam repertoire: four recordings worldwide, of
which none are from the classical Egyptian or Levantine tradition. The recordings that
would let a listener actually hear what a maqam is are in collections like yours.

Would the foundation consider permitting a small number of excerpts, from recordings
whose rights you control, to be streamed within this map? Each would carry the
performer, the maqam, the approximate date, and a credit to AMAR with a link to your
site. I am not asking for files to redistribute, and the project carries no
advertising and sells nothing.

I would also welcome your view on which recordings in your holdings are already clear
of copyright and freely usable, if any are.

With respect for the work you do,

Hamza

## 3. CREM / CNRS — Musée de l'Homme sound archives

**Route:** archives.crem-cnrs.fr. The archive runs on Telemeta and many items are
already listed publicly with per-item access conditions. Contact via the CREM at
Université Paris Nanterre (LESC-CREM).

**Subject:** Demande d'autorisation: extraits d'enregistrements pour une carte sonore non commerciale

Bonjour,

Je réalise Sawt, une carte sonore en ligne, non commerciale et sans publicité, des
traditions musicales et dévotionnelles du monde musulman. L'auditeur clique sur un
lieu et entend un enregistrement accompagné d'une courte notice sur la tradition, le
mode et l'interprète lorsque celui-ci est connu.

Les archives sonores du CNRS et du Musée de l'Homme contiennent des enregistrements de
terrain du Maghreb, du Moyen-Orient et d'Asie centrale qui n'ont pas d'équivalent dans
les collections sous licence ouverte. Je pense en particulier aux enregistrements de
musique modale et de confréries soufies.

Serait-il possible de diffuser en streaming un nombre limité d'extraits dans le cadre
de ce projet, avec mention du CREM, du CNRS et du Musée de l'Homme, du collecteur, et
un lien vers la notice Telemeta de chaque item? Je ne demande pas à télécharger ni à
redistribuer les fichiers.

Si une convention ou des droits s'appliquent, je serais heureux d'en connaître les
modalités.

Avec mes remerciements,

Hamza

## 4. Bibliothèque nationale de France — 1932 Cairo Congress recordings

**Route:** BnF, département de l'Audiovisuel. Use the "Poser une question à un
bibliothécaire" (SINDBAD) service on bnf.fr, addressed to the Audiovisuel department.

**Subject:** Demande d'autorisation: enregistrements du Congrès du Caire (1932)

Bonjour,

Je réalise Sawt, une carte sonore en ligne non commerciale consacrée aux traditions
musicales du monde musulman.

Je m'intéresse aux enregistrements réalisés lors du Congrès de musique arabe du Caire
en 1932, dont la BnF conserve une partie. Ce corpus est sans doute le document le plus
important qui existe sur l'état des traditions modales arabes, turques et persanes à
cette date, et il est précisément ce qui manque aux collections ouvertes.

Serait-il possible de diffuser quelques extraits en streaming, avec mention de la BnF
et un lien vers la notice de chaque enregistrement? Le projet ne comporte aucune
publicité et ne vend rien.

Je serais également reconnaissant de savoir quels enregistrements de ce corpus sont
aujourd'hui dans le domaine public.

Avec mes remerciements,

Hamza

## 5. ICCR / CCRT — Chishti qawwali recordings on archive.org

**Route:** Indian Council for Cultural Relations (iccr.gov.in) and the Centre for
Cultural Resources and Training (ccrtindia.gov.in). The relevant uploads appear on
archive.org under the `dni.ncaa` identifiers.

**Subject:** Permission request: qawwali recordings for a non-commercial heritage map

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am building Sawt, a non-commercial online map of Islamic musical and devotional
traditions. Listeners click a place on a globe and hear a recording from it, with a
short written account of the tradition and the performers.

I have found recordings of Chishti qawwali, including performances by the Niazi
Brothers, uploaded to archive.org under identifiers associated with the ICCR and the
CCRT. The upload pages do not state a licence, so I have not used them, and I would
rather ask than assume.

Would the Council permit a small number of these recordings to be streamed within the
map, credited to the ICCR with a link to the source page? The project carries no
advertising, sells nothing, and exists to make these traditions audible to people who
would otherwise never encounter them. Delhi is one of the seven centres the map is
built around, and at present I have almost nothing from the Chishti tradition to place
there.

If permission rests with another body, I would be grateful to be pointed in the right
direction.

With thanks,

Hamza
